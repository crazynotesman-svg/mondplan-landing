The eight moon phases

The lunar cycle - a synodic month of 29.53 days - runs through eight primary phases.

  1. New Moon

    The moon is between Earth and the Sun; the lit side faces away from us. Traditional time for rest and planning.

  2. Waxing Crescent

    A thin sliver grows on the right. Growth energy is traditionally considered to be rising.

  3. First Quarter

    Half of the disc is lit. A traditional turning point in the cycle toward growth.

  4. Waxing Gibbous

    More than half lit, still growing. Good time for planting and transplanting in traditional practice.

  5. Full Moon

    The whole disc is lit. Traditionally favored for harvesting and gathering.

  6. Waning Gibbous

    Light begins to recede from the right. Traditionally a settling, pruning time.

  7. Last Quarter

    Half lit again, waning side. Traditional end-of-growth turning point.

  8. Waning Crescent

    A thin sliver remains on the left. Time for pruning, weeding and soil work.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers about the 2026 moon phases, the synodic month and how the calendar is computed.

When is the next full moon in 2026?

The full moons in 2026 are: Jan 3, Feb 1, Mar 3 (total lunar eclipse), Apr 2, May 1, May 31, Jun 29, Jul 29, Aug 28 (partial lunar eclipse), Sep 26, Oct 26, Nov 24 and Dec 24. See the calendar below for exact UTC and CET times.

What is a synodic month?

A synodic month is the time between two identical lunar phases - on average 29.53059 days (about 29 days, 12 hours, 44 minutes). It is the period on which a lunar calendar is built.

How do moon phases affect gardening?

In biodynamic tradition the waxing moon (new moon to full moon) is favored for sowing, planting and transplanting, the full moon for harvesting, and the waning moon (full moon to new moon) for pruning, weeding and soil work. These are traditional guidelines, not scientific claims.

Are the moon phase times in this calendar only valid for Germany?

The exact instants (UTC) are valid worldwide. The table additionally shows Central European Time (CET/CEST) for readers in Germany, Austria and Switzerland. In other time zones, use the UTC column and add your local offset.

How is this moon phase calendar calculated?

The dates are computed with the standard algorithm from Jean Meeus, 'Astronomical Algorithms' (chapter 49, Phases of the Moon), with the main correction terms - accuracy of about ±1-2 minutes. The results were cross-checked against the 2026 solar and lunar eclipse dates published by NASA.
